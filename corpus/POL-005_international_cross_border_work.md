# International and Cross-Border Work Policy

Document ID: POL-005
Owner: People Team (Global Mobility), with Legal, Payroll Tax, and Information Security
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Applies to: All regular employees of Lumora Systems, Inc. who wish to work temporarily from a location other than their approved work location.

> Lumora Systems, Inc. is a fictional company. This document is synthetic and was created for an educational AI engineering project.

## 1. Purpose

Working from another state or country, even for a short time, can create legal, tax, immigration, and security obligations for both the employee and Lumora. These include state income tax withholding, "permanent establishment" corporate tax risk, social security obligations, work authorization (visa) requirements, data protection rules, and export control restrictions. This policy explains the limits on temporary work from another location, the approvals required, and the process for requesting an exception. It supplements the Remote and Hybrid Work Policy (POL-004).

## 2. Definitions

- **Approved work location:** the office or home address recorded for the employee in PeopleHub.
- **Temporary remote work:** performing any work (including checking email or attending meetings) while physically located somewhere other than the approved work location, for a limited period, without a permanent change of location.
- **Workday:** any day on which the employee performs any work, regardless of the number of hours.
- **Approved country list:** the list of countries maintained by Legal and Information Security from which temporary remote work may be requested (section 5.2).

## 3. Domestic Temporary Remote Work (Within the Employee's Home Country)

### 3.1 Same state or province

Working temporarily from another location within the same US state or Canadian province (or within England for UK employees) requires only that the employee inform their manager and follow the security requirements in section 6.

### 3.2 Another US state or Canadian province

US employees may work temporarily from another US state for up to 20 workdays (4 weeks) per calendar year in total, with manager approval requested in PeopleHub at least 10 business days in advance. Canadian employees may work from another Canadian province on the same terms.

Requests that would exceed 20 workdays in another state in a calendar year require an additional review by the People Team and Payroll Tax before approval. Payroll Tax will determine whether income tax withholding must be set up for the other state; many states impose withholding obligations after a threshold number of days, and some (such as New York) apply a "convenience of the employer" rule. If the stay will exceed 60 workdays, it is treated as a change of approved work location under POL-004 section 9.

## 4. International Temporary Remote Work ("Work From Abroad")

### 4.1 Allowance

Eligible employees may work temporarily from an approved country outside their employing entity's country for up to **20 workdays in any rolling 12-month period**. The 20 workdays may be taken in one block or several shorter periods. Days spent abroad on approved business travel for Lumora do not count against this allowance; days of PTO taken while abroad do not count either, but any day on which the employee performs any work counts as a workday.

### 4.2 Eligibility

To be eligible for international temporary remote work, an employee must:

1. Have completed at least 6 months of continuous employment with Lumora.
2. Have a most recent performance rating of "Meets Expectations" or higher.
3. Hold a role that does not require physical presence (see POL-004 section 3.2).
4. Be a citizen of, or otherwise legally permitted to enter, the destination country as a visitor. Employees are responsible for confirming that their passport and visa status allow entry; Lumora does not sponsor visas for temporary remote work.
5. Not be in a role with "Restricted" data access unless Information Security has granted a specific approval (section 6.3).

Interns and contractors are not eligible.

### 4.3 Advance notice and approvals

Requests must be submitted through the "Work From Abroad" form in PeopleHub at least **30 calendar days** before the first day of work abroad. The request requires approval, in order, from:

1. The employee's manager.
2. The People Team (Global Mobility), which checks the approved country list, the 20-workday allowance, and immigration considerations.
3. Information Security, which confirms device, VPN, and data access requirements.

Employees may not begin working abroad until all three approvals are recorded. Requests submitted with less than 30 days' notice will be reviewed only if the manager provides a business justification, and may be declined solely due to insufficient notice.

## 5. Destination Rules

### 5.1 Time zone and availability

Employees working abroad must remain available for at least 3 hours of overlap with their team's core collaboration hours (POL-004 section 6.1). If the destination time zone makes this impossible, the request will be declined.

### 5.2 Approved country list

As of 2026, temporary remote work may be requested from the following countries: all European Union member states (including Portugal, Spain, France, Germany, Italy, Ireland, and the Netherlands), the United Kingdom, Canada, the United States, Mexico, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, and Singapore. Requests for other countries are evaluated case by case and require Legal review. Temporary remote work is never permitted from countries subject to comprehensive US, UK, EU, or Canadian sanctions or embargoes, or from countries designated "high-risk" by Information Security.

### 5.3 Countries where Lumora has an entity

Working from a country where Lumora has an employing entity (the US, Canada, or the UK) is still subject to the 20-workday allowance when the employee is employed by a different entity. For example, a US employee working from London for three weeks uses 15 workdays of the allowance.

## 6. Security Requirements for Working Away From the Approved Location

### 6.1 Devices and network

Employees must use only Lumora-managed devices, keep devices with them or locked in a secure location at all times, and connect through the Lumora VPN for all work. Public Wi-Fi may be used only through the VPN. Printing Company documents at hotels, co-working spaces, or other shared facilities is prohibited.

### 6.2 Data access

Information Security may restrict access to certain systems from outside the employee's home country to comply with data protection laws, customer contracts, and export controls. Employees with "Confidential" data access must not download Confidential data to local storage while abroad.

### 6.3 Restricted data roles

Employees whose PeopleHub profile shows "Restricted" data access (for example, those with production database administrator rights, payroll data access, or access to customer encryption keys) may work abroad only with a specific written approval from Information Security, which may require use of a travel loaner laptop and may temporarily suspend Restricted access during the stay.

## 7. Exceptions (Global Mobility Exception)

Requests that exceed the limits in sections 3 and 4 (for example, working from another country for six consecutive weeks, which is approximately 30 workdays and exceeds the 20-workday allowance) require a Global Mobility exception. To request an exception, the employee submits the Work From Abroad form with the "exception requested" box checked and a written business justification. Exceptions require approval from the employee's department head, the VP of People, and Legal, after a tax and immigration assessment, which may require an external advisor and take 4 to 6 weeks. Exceptions are granted rarely, are never guaranteed, and are generally limited to compelling personal circumstances (for example, caring for a seriously ill family member) or business needs. An alternative that is often approved is to combine the 20-workday allowance with PTO for the remaining days, so that no work is performed abroad beyond the allowance.

## 8. Tax and Legal Considerations

- Employees remain on the payroll of their employing entity during temporary remote work and continue to observe that entity's holidays (POL-003 section 9).
- Employees are personally responsible for any individual tax filings that arise from their personal choice to work from another location, unless Payroll Tax determines that Lumora must withhold.
- Lumora does not provide personal tax or immigration advice. Employees should consult their own advisors.
- Business travel (attending a customer meeting or conference at Lumora's request) is governed by the Travel and Expense Policy (POL-006) and is not temporary remote work.

## 9. Expenses

Lumora does not reimburse travel, accommodation, or other costs associated with temporary remote work, because it is undertaken for the employee's personal benefit. Costs for business travel are reimbursed under POL-006.

## 10. Non-Compliance

Working from another state or country without the required approval exposes Lumora to legal and tax risk and is a violation of this policy. Unapproved work from abroad may result in disciplinary action, loss of remote work eligibility, and recovery of any resulting tax penalties where permitted by law.
