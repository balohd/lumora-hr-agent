# Code of Conduct and Workplace Behavior Policy

Document ID: POL-001
Owner: People Team (Employee Relations)
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Applies to: All Lumora Systems, Inc. employees, interns, and contractors in all locations

> Lumora Systems, Inc. is a fictional company. This document is synthetic and was created for an educational AI engineering project.

## 1. Purpose

Lumora Systems, Inc. ("Lumora" or "the Company") is committed to a workplace where every person is treated with dignity and respect, where people can raise concerns without fear, and where we conduct business honestly and lawfully. This Code of Conduct describes the behavior Lumora expects from everyone who works with us and explains how to report concerns. It is the foundation for all other Lumora policies. Where a more specific policy exists (for example, the Information Security Policy, POL-007, or the HR Case Handling and Escalation Procedure, POL-012), that policy provides additional detail and should be read together with this Code.

## 2. Scope

This Code applies to all employees (full-time and part-time), interns, temporary workers, and independent contractors, in every Lumora office (Austin, Denver, Toronto, and London) and when working remotely. It applies during working hours, at Company-sponsored events (including offsites, holiday parties, and team dinners), on Company systems and communication channels (email, chat, video meetings), and on social media when the conduct relates to Lumora, its people, or its customers.

## 3. Core Principles

Everyone at Lumora is expected to:

- Treat colleagues, candidates, customers, and partners with respect and professionalism.
- Act with integrity: be honest in our records, our reports, and our communications.
- Protect Company and customer information as described in the Information Security Policy (POL-007).
- Comply with applicable laws and regulations in every country where we operate.
- Speak up when something seems wrong, and support others who speak up.
- Avoid conflicts of interest, or disclose them promptly when they cannot be avoided.

Managers carry an additional responsibility: they must model these principles, create an environment in which concerns can be raised, and promptly escalate any report of misconduct they receive to the People Team. A manager may not investigate a report of harassment, discrimination, or retaliation on their own.

## 4. Equal Employment Opportunity and Non-Discrimination

Lumora provides equal employment opportunity to all employees and applicants. Employment decisions (recruiting, hiring, compensation, promotion, discipline, and termination) are made without regard to race, color, religion, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, pregnancy, national origin, ancestry, age, disability, genetic information, marital status, veteran or military status, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law. Lumora provides reasonable accommodations to qualified individuals with disabilities and for sincerely held religious beliefs, unless an accommodation would create an undue hardship. Accommodation requests are handled by the People Team under the HR Case Handling and Escalation Procedure (POL-012) and are treated as confidential.

## 5. Anti-Harassment

### 5.1 Prohibited conduct

Harassment of any kind is prohibited. Harassment includes unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that is severe or pervasive enough to create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment. Examples include slurs, offensive jokes, name-calling, threats, intimidation, ridicule, insults, offensive images or objects, and interference with work performance. Lumora's standard is stricter than the legal standard: we prohibit inappropriate conduct even if it would not rise to the level of unlawful harassment.

### 5.2 Sexual harassment

Sexual harassment includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal, visual, or physical conduct of a sexual nature. It includes quid pro quo situations, in which employment decisions are linked to submission to sexual conduct, and hostile environment situations. Romantic or sexual relationships between a manager and anyone in their reporting line are prohibited; if such a relationship develops, it must be disclosed to the People Team so that reporting lines can be changed.

### 5.3 Bullying and abusive conduct

Repeated, unreasonable behavior directed toward a person or group that creates a risk to health and safety, such as persistent belittling, public humiliation, sabotage of work, or exclusion, is prohibited regardless of whether it relates to a protected characteristic.

## 6. Retaliation Is Prohibited

Lumora strictly prohibits retaliation against anyone who, in good faith, reports a concern, participates in an investigation, requests an accommodation, or opposes conduct they reasonably believe is unlawful or violates this Code. Retaliation includes termination, demotion, unfavorable schedule changes, exclusion from meetings, negative reviews motivated by the report, threats, and any other action that would discourage a reasonable person from raising a concern. Retaliation is treated as a serious (Severity 1) matter under POL-012 and will result in discipline up to and including termination.

## 7. Workplace Safety and Violence Prevention

Lumora does not tolerate threats, threatening behavior, or acts of violence. Weapons are not permitted on Company premises or at Company events, except where applicable law requires otherwise. If anyone is in immediate danger, call local emergency services first (911 in the United States and Canada, 999 in the United Kingdom), and then notify Workplace Security and the People Team. Concerns about potential violence, including statements of intent to harm oneself or others, must be reported immediately and will be escalated as Severity 1 under POL-012.

## 8. Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest arises when personal interests interfere, or appear to interfere, with an employee's duty to Lumora. Employees must disclose potential conflicts through the Conflict of Interest disclosure form in PeopleHub. Common examples include:

- Outside employment or consulting for a competitor, customer, or vendor.
- A financial interest of more than 1% in a competitor, customer, or vendor.
- Hiring, supervising, or making purchasing decisions involving a relative or partner.
- Serving on the board of a for-profit company (requires prior approval by the General Counsel).

Outside employment that does not compete with Lumora and does not interfere with an employee's job performance is generally permitted, but must still be disclosed if it involves any technology field in which Lumora operates.

## 9. Gifts, Meals, and Entertainment

Employees may accept occasional gifts, meals, or entertainment from business partners only if they are modest, customary, and not intended to influence a business decision. Gifts with a value of more than US $100 (or local equivalent) from a single source in a calendar year must be declined or reported to the Legal team. Cash and cash equivalents (gift cards, vouchers) may never be accepted. Gifts to government officials require prior written approval from Legal. Entertainment provided to customers is subject to the Travel and Expense Policy (POL-006).

## 10. Use of Company Resources and Communications

Company systems, equipment, and accounts are provided for business purposes. Limited personal use is acceptable if it does not interfere with work, violate any policy, or expose the Company to security risk. Employees have no expectation of privacy in Company systems to the extent permitted by local law. Communications on Company channels must be professional. Use of Company equipment is further described in the Equipment and Home Office Policy (POL-011), and acceptable use of systems is described in POL-007.

## 11. Social Media and Public Statements

Employees may not speak on behalf of Lumora publicly unless authorized by Communications. When discussing Lumora on personal social media, employees should make clear that the views are their own, must not disclose Confidential or Restricted information, and must not harass or disparage colleagues. Nothing in this policy restricts employees from discussing terms and conditions of employment or engaging in other activity protected by law.

## 12. How to Report a Concern

Employees have several channels for raising concerns, and may use whichever feels most comfortable:

1. Their manager, or any other manager they trust.
2. The People Team, by email to people@lumora.example or by opening a case in PeopleHub.
3. Employee Relations directly, at er@lumora.example, for sensitive matters such as harassment, discrimination, or retaliation.
4. The Lumora Ethics Line, an independent third-party hotline available 24/7 by phone or web, which accepts anonymous reports where local law permits.

Reports should include as much detail as possible: what happened, when, where, who was involved, and whether there were witnesses. Employees do not need to have all the facts or proof before reporting. The HR assistant chatbot can help an employee understand which channel to use and can prepare a draft case summary, but it does not investigate or decide on misconduct matters; see POL-012 section 6.

## 13. Investigations

The People Team (or, for certain matters, Legal or an external investigator) will review every report promptly, fairly, and as confidentially as possible. Confidentiality will be maintained to the extent consistent with conducting a thorough investigation and complying with law; complete anonymity cannot always be guaranteed. Employees are required to cooperate with investigations and to answer questions truthfully. Interim measures, such as schedule changes or temporary changes to reporting lines, may be put in place during an investigation to protect those involved.

## 14. Consequences of Violations

Violations of this Code may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract, as well as legal action where appropriate. Disciplinary steps typically follow a progression of verbal warning, written warning, final written warning, and termination, but Lumora may skip steps for serious misconduct. Knowingly making a false report is itself a violation of this Code; a report made in good faith that turns out to be unfounded is not.

## 15. Acknowledgment and Training

All employees must acknowledge this Code during onboarding (see POL-010) and annually thereafter. All employees must complete Respectful Workplace training within 30 days of hire and every two years thereafter; managers complete an additional manager module. Completion is tracked in PeopleHub.

## 16. Questions

Questions about this Code should be directed to the People Team at people@lumora.example. This policy is reviewed annually by the People Team and Legal.
